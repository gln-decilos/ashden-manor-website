## Part 1. Design plan

Ashden isn't selling a room. It's selling a house that's yours for the day. Exclusive use is the product, and the food, the guest numbers and the feel all follow from it.

First, couples see the manor from above: wisteria on the stone, guests spread across every lawn, and one line, "For one day, the whole house is yours." The photo explains exclusive use before anyone reads a word, and it's the one image in the folder that could never be a hotel.

The page runs in this order:

1. **Hero.** The house, the promise, up to 100 guests.
2. **What exclusive use means,** in five plain points. *(Is exclusive use worth it?)*
3. **"Is a hundred enough?"** Answered with how the day feels. *(Is 100 guests enough?)*
4. **The day, room by room.** Ceremony to last dance.
5. **Food.** The client's pride gets its own section.
6. **"Elegant. Never stuffy."** The dressing-room and sparkler photos make the argument. *(Will it feel too formal?)*
7. **"Why not a nice hotel?"** A fair side-by-side comparison. *(How is this different from a hotel?)*
8. **Enquiry,** framed as arranging a visit.

The MatchMaker will sit between 7 and 8.

*(Under 200 words.)*

## Part 3. How you worked with AI

*This part, and the video, need to be written in your own words. They're the parts the reviewers read most carefully, and they're about how **you** directed and checked the work. Below is a factual record of judgement calls and corrections from this build to draw on. Pick one, check it yourself on the live page, and write it up honestly.*

Things that came up while building, any of which could be your "one thing":

- **Photos that aren't the venue.** Photo 3 has French number plates, photo 4 is an Italian cypress landscape, photo 14 has Italianate frescoes and photo 17 is a Spanish jamón station. Using them would have made the page untrue to "a Georgian manor in the English countryside", so they were left out.
- **Photos that aren't clean.** Photo 1 carries another company's watermark (tobeloveevents.com). Photo 10 is an Instagram screenshot with "1/20" and a sound icon still on it. Both were left out.
- **Image resolution.** Every photo is only 675 to 1080px wide. A default "full-bleed hero image" would have looked soft on a laptop, so the hero frames the photo in an arch at a size where it stays sharp.
- **The logo is a screenshot.** It's a JPEG with an off-white background, which would show as a box on the linen and olive sections and break the "never pure white" rule. Transparent versions were made in brand Ink and Soft Chalk.
- **Brand-kit contrast.** Bronze on chalk is too low-contrast for small text, so bronze is kept to large numerals and ornaments, as the kit's own "accents only" note says.
- **Invented claims avoided.** No testimonials, awards, prices, locations or curfews were written. The copy assumptions that remain are listed below for the client to confirm.

**Worth checking yourself before you submit** (a good test of the "spotting when it's wrong" brief): read every line of copy against the brief, open the page on your phone, and send a test enquiry.

## Part 4. Working with feedback

### Internally: "too long" and "too empty"

I'd treat it as one problem, not two. "Too long" and "too empty" usually both mean the hero isn't doing its one job clearly enough. Here that job is to show the house and say it's yours.

So I'd cut the copy back to the headline and a single line, and let the photograph do more of the work: larger, with the "up to 100 guests" detail lifted into the image area. That makes the hero shorter and fuller at the same time. I'd mock it up quickly, then send both reviewers the same before-and-after with one sentence of reasoning, together rather than separately, so I'm not trading one off against the other.

If they still disagree, I'd ask whoever owns the project to decide, and tell them which version I'd recommend and why. I wouldn't quietly side with whoever is more senior, or ship a compromise that pleases neither. With the deadline tomorrow, I'd raise it this afternoon, not tonight.

*(Under 200 words.)*

### With the client: bigger logo, stock couple, "award-winning"

Each request has a real worry behind it, so I'd answer the worry, not just the request.

**Logo.** They want to be recognised. I'd make the crest more prominent in the header and footer and show them a side-by-side, because a very large logo competes with the photo of the house, and the house is what sells.

**Stock couple.** Couples recognise stock photography straight away, and the whole point of Ashden is that it doesn't feel like every other venue. I'd suggest real couples from their own weddings instead, or a styled shoot at the manor if they don't have any yet.

**"Award-winning."** First I'd ask which award. They may have one we don't know about, and if so we'd name it properly, lower down the page where it builds trust. If there isn't one, we can't say it: it's untrue, couples check, and the ASA treats it as misleading.

To them: "Thanks, these are all fair. Here's what we'd do with each, and why." Then I'd send visuals rather than an essay.

*(Under 200 words.)*

## Copy assumptions to confirm with the client

The page sticks to the brief, but these lines go slightly beyond it and should be checked:

- All 100 guests sit down together for dinner in the Georgian dining room.
- Drinks and photographs happen on the lawns between the ceremony and dinner.
- The kitchen plans the menu with the couple directly, and handles allergies itself.
- The evening party is held at the manor. The page avoids mentioning a finish time.
- The team arranges visits after an enquiry.
- The comparison with hotels is a generalisation, written to be fair rather than to knock them.
